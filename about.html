About PedalHound

Faceless, independent, and not for sale. Just honest gear reviews to help you spend your money wisely.

🐾
The Deal

Faceless. Independent. Honest.

PedalHound is a faceless, independent guitar gear review site. No sponsored content, no brand deals, no BS. Just honest breakdowns of pedals, amps, and accessories to help you spend your money wisely.

It's run by a guitarist who got tired of reading biased reviews — the kind where every pedal is "incredible" and every amp is "studio-quality," because the person writing it has an affiliate link or a free unit to protect.

There's no name here, no face, no social media presence to maintain. That's intentional. The gear is the story, not the reviewer.

How It Works

The Review Process

The same approach on every piece of gear, no exceptions.

🎸

Played Before Written

Nothing gets published until it's been played for at least two weeks across multiple contexts — practice, rehearsal, recording. Usually longer.

⚖️

No Paid Coverage

No manufacturer pays for a review slot or a favorable score. If gear is sent for review, that's disclosed clearly in the article. The score is always independent.

🔍

Scores Are Relative

A great overdrive gets a great score because I've played a lot of overdrives. Every rating is a comparison, not an absolute. Context is always given.

💬

Plain Language

Reviews are written for players, not spec sheets. If a pedal sounds "harmonically complex," I'll also tell you what music it's actually suited for.

🔄

Reviews Get Updated

If a firmware update changes a pedal, or more time with it changes my opinion, the review gets updated. Old impressions don't stick around if they're no longer accurate.

🎯

The Full Scale Gets Used

Scores run 1–10 and every number means something. A 5 is average. A 9 is genuinely excellent. Not everything gets an 8.5 to avoid controversy.

Why Anonymous?

A Word on the Format

Gear review culture has a personality problem. Too many sites are built around the reviewer's image — their YouTube persona, their Instagram feed, their affiliate deals. The gear becomes secondary to the content creator.

Staying anonymous sidesteps all of that. There's no follower count to protect, no brand deal to honor, no reason to be diplomatic about a pedal that genuinely isn't good. The writing has to stand on its own because there's no face to give it credibility by association.

If you disagree with a review, argue the gear — not the person. That's the whole idea.
