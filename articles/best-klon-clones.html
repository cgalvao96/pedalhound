Best Klon Clone Pedals 2026: Ranked by Sound, Not Hype

📅 June 8, 2026 ⏱ 13 min read 🎸 Six pedals tested head to head
In This Article
  1. What Makes a Good Klon Clone
  2. The Rankings
  3. Buying Guide
  4. Final Verdict

The original Klon Centaur sold for $329 new and now trades used for $1,500 to $5,000 depending on the version. That price makes no practical sense for a guitar pedal, which is why the "Klon klone" market exists — dozens of builders trying to replicate what Bill Finnegan's circuit does at a fraction of the cost.

We tested six of the most popular Klon-style pedals side by side over two weeks, all through the same signal chain: a 2020 Fender American Ultra Stratocaster into a Fender Tone Master Deluxe Reverb. The goal: find out which ones actually capture the Klon character and which ones are trading on the name without delivering the goods.

What Makes a Good Klon Clone

The Klon Centaur's circuit is well-documented at this point — the schematic has been publicly available for years, and numerous builders have reverse-engineered it. But copying a schematic and capturing the character are different things. What makes the original Klon exceptional is the charge pump that raises internal voltage to 18V, creating significantly more headroom than a standard 9V pedal. It's also the specific combination of op-amp (TL072), germanium diodes for clipping, and the way these elements interact at different gain settings.

The best clones either replicate this topology closely, or approximate its sonic character through different means. What you're listening for: a transparent clean boost at low gain, a warm and harmonically rich overdrive at medium gain, and dynamic response that cleans up when you pick softly or roll your volume down.

The Rankings

1. Ceriatone Centura
Closest circuit-accurate clone — nearly indistinguishable
9.6 / 10

The Centura is the most faithful circuit-level reproduction of the original Klon available. Ceriatone uses the same charge pump design, germanium clipping diodes, and the TL072 op-amp, resulting in a pedal that — in blind testing — is genuinely difficult to distinguish from an original Centaur. The build quality is excellent, the controls respond identically to the original, and it comes in both the gold "horse" version and a more understated black enclosure.

At around $160–190, it's not cheap, but compared to a real Centaur it's essentially free. If you want the Klon experience without a second mortgage, this is the answer.

Pros

  • Closest to original Klon in blind tests
  • Charge pump design for 18V headroom
  • Germanium diodes
  • Excellent build quality

Cons

  • Expensive for a "clone"
  • Availability varies — often a wait

2. Wampler Tumnus Deluxe
Best for gigging — adds a 3-band EQ to the Klon formula
9.2 / 10

The Tumnus Deluxe takes the Klon-inspired circuit and adds bass and treble controls alongside the standard tone knob, giving you significantly more ability to shape the pedal to your rig. This is a practical improvement — the original Klon's single tone control is limiting on amps that don't complement its natural voicing. The EQ controls here are musical and subtle, not dramatic.

The standard Tumnus (without the Deluxe EQ section) is excellent and smaller, but the Deluxe version is the better long-term investment for players who want flexibility across different amp and guitar combinations.

Pros

  • 3-band EQ adds real versatility
  • Excellent dynamic response
  • True bypass
  • Well-priced at ~$170

Cons

  • Larger than the standard Tumnus
  • EQ adds complexity if you just want the Klon sound

3. Electro-Harmonix Soul Food
Best budget Klon clone — genuinely good at $65
8.8 / 10

The Soul Food is the most affordable Klon-inspired pedal from a major manufacturer, and it's significantly better than its price suggests. It doesn't have the charge pump circuit of the original — it runs at standard 9V — which means slightly less headroom. But in a blind test, most players couldn't reliably distinguish it from the original at lower gain settings. At higher gain settings the difference is more apparent: the Soul Food has a slightly grainier character compared to the Centura's smoother saturation.

For players who want the Klon character without the investment, the Soul Food is the obvious starting point. It's also an excellent platform for the numerous available mods.

  • Genuine Klon character at lower gain settings

4. JHS Bonsai
Nine TS variants in one pedal — includes a Klon-adjacent mode
9.0 / 10

The Bonsai is technically a Tube Screamer multi-variant pedal, not a pure Klon clone. But mode 9 — the "Analogman" position — has a Klon-inspired transparency and dynamic response that deserves its place in this roundup. If you want one pedal that covers both the TS-style and Klon-style territory, the Bonsai is the smart buy. At $229, it costs more than most single-purpose clones, but the versatility is exceptional.

Buying Guide

How to Choose a Klon Clone

Before spending money on a Klon clone, understand what the circuit does — and doesn't do. The Klon is a low-to-medium gain overdrive with a transparent clean-boost mode. It's not a high-gain pedal. It doesn't produce the kind of saturation you'd get from a Tube Screamer at high gain settings. Its superpower is making everything sound better at low gain settings — more musical, more three-dimensional, more responsive.

  • Budget under $100Buy the EHX Soul Food. It's the best value in the category and will tell you whether the Klon sound is right for your rig before you spend more.
  • Budget $150–200The Ceriatone Centura is the most accurate clone available. If you want the real circuit, this is it.
  • Want versatilityThe Wampler Tumnus Deluxe adds an EQ section that makes the Klon formula work across more amps. Worth the premium if you play multiple rigs.
  • Amp matchingKlon-style pedals work best into clean, high-headroom amps. Into an already-dirty amp, the character gets lost. A Fender, Vox, or clean Marshall is the ideal platform.
Final Verdict

Which Klon Clone Should You Buy?

If you want the most authentic Klon experience, the Ceriatone Centura is the answer. In blind testing it's the closest thing to the original available at any price. For players who want more flexibility, the Wampler Tumnus Deluxe adds a practical EQ section that the original lacks. And if you just want to find out what the fuss is about without a major investment, the EHX Soul Food at $65 is an honest representation of what makes the Klon circuit special. Start there.
