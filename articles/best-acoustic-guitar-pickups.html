Amplifying an acoustic guitar faithfully is one of the hardest challenges in guitar audio. An acoustic guitar's sound is produced by the entire instrument — the top resonating, the air moving inside the body, the wood vibrating. Capturing that complexity through a pickup and reproducing it through a PA or amplifier requires understanding what kind of pickup technology best serves your playing style, your guitar, and your performance context.

We tested five acoustic pickups across different guitars and performance situations, evaluating natural tone, feedback resistance, installation requirements, and value. Whether you're a fingerpicker who needs detailed harmonic response or a strummer who needs reliable volume in loud stage environments, there's a pickup on this list that's right for you.

Types of Acoustic Guitar Pickups

Undersaddle transducers are the most common factory-installed pickup type. They sit under the saddle in the bridge and sense string vibration through pressure. They're very feedback-resistant — ideal for loud stage environments — but can sound thin and "quacky" because they don't capture the body resonance. Budget undersaddle pickups are responsible for much of the bad acoustic amplification sound people associate with live acoustic performance.

Soundhole pickups mount in the guitar's soundhole like an electric guitar pickup and capture string vibration magnetically. They're easy to install and remove without modifying the guitar, and they deliver warm, guitar-like tone that works well at high stage volumes. The tradeoff is that they don't capture acoustic body resonance — they sound like a thinline electric rather than a true acoustic.

Internal microphones are mounted inside the guitar body and capture the actual acoustic resonance of the instrument. The result is the most natural, three-dimensional acoustic tone available — but microphones are more prone to feedback at high stage volumes and usually require permanent installation.

Contact transducers (piezo dots) are glued to the inside of the guitar top, sensing vibration directly from the wood. K&K Sound's Pure Mini is the most respected example. They capture body resonance better than undersaddle designs and have less feedback risk than microphones, occupying a useful middle ground.

What to Look for in an Acoustic Pickup

  • Natural acoustic character: The best pickup sounds like your guitar amplified, not like an electric guitar or a thin, harsh piezo approximation. Listen for full harmonic response and body resonance.
  • Feedback resistance: For live performance, you need to manage stage volume without the pickup feeding back. Undersaddle and soundhole pickups handle feedback best; internal microphones are most challenging in loud environments.
  • Installation requirements: Some pickups (soundhole designs) require no modification. Others (undersaddle, internal mic) require professional installation with potential guitar modification. Factor in installation cost when comparing prices.
  • Passive vs active: Active pickups include a preamp and require battery power. They produce higher output, better signal, and often more tonal control. Passive pickups are simpler but may need a preamp DI box for best results.
  • Guitar compatibility: Soundhole pickups require a standard-size soundhole. Some guitars (classical, parlor, smaller body guitars) may need different dimensions. Always verify compatibility before buying.

The Rankings

1. LR Baggs Lyric Acoustic Microphone
Best premium internal mic, the most natural-sounding acoustic pickup available

The LR Baggs Lyric is the gold standard for acoustic guitar pickups. It's an internal microphone pickup mounted inside the guitar near the sound hole, designed to capture the actual acoustic resonance of the instrument rather than simply sensing string vibration. The result is a pickup that sounds like a close-miked acoustic recording — full, three-dimensional, with the natural body bloom and harmonic richness that makes acoustic guitars sound beautiful.

LR Baggs's signal processing technology (DSP-controlled preamp) works to extend the feedback headroom beyond what a traditional internal microphone could achieve, making the Lyric practical in live performance situations where a conventional mic would feed back immediately. It requires professional installation, which adds to the cost (~$200 for the pickup, plus installation). But the result is genuinely remarkable: plug your acoustic guitar into any DI box and sound like you're being professionally miked in a studio. For acoustic players who perform live and refuse to compromise on natural tone, the Lyric is the benchmark.

2. K&K Pure Mini Internal Pickup
Best transducer pickup, three-point contact for exceptional body resonance capture

The K&K Pure Mini is the most beloved acoustic pickup among fingerpickers and acoustic purists for one clear reason: it captures the acoustic character of the guitar better than any pickup at its price. Three small transducers are glued to the inside of the guitar's top (bridge plate), and their combined output captures the actual vibration of the wood. The result is a warm, full, naturally acoustic sound with the body resonance and harmonic complexity that microphones capture but undersaddle pickups miss.

The Pure Mini is passive — it has no battery, no preamp, just three transducers sending signal through a single output. This simplicity is a feature: nothing in the chain to color the sound, nothing to go wrong, no batteries to die mid-performance. The tradeoff is that you need a quality preamp DI box to bring the signal up and shape it. LR Baggs Para DI is a popular pairing. At around $65-80 for the pickup alone, the K&K is exceptional value. Installation requires a guitar tech (gluing inside the guitar), but many players consider it a permanent upgrade they keep across all their guitars.

3. LR Baggs M1 Active Soundhole Pickup
Best soundhole pickup, magnetic pickup with body sensor for exceptional acoustic character

The LR Baggs M1 Active soundhole pickup is the most sophisticated soundhole pickup design available. Unlike standard magnetic soundhole pickups that only sense string vibration, the M1 Active includes a body sensor that also captures guitar top resonance and blends it with the magnetic string signal. The result sounds significantly more acoustic and three-dimensional than conventional soundhole designs — you get the body bloom and warmth that makes an acoustic guitar sound like an acoustic guitar.

The M1 Active's preamp circuit is powered by the standard 9V battery housed in the pickup body. Installation is a simple snap-in fit for most dreadnought and 000-style guitars — no guitar modification required. At around $179, it's priced at the top of the soundhole pickup category, but the acoustic character it delivers justifies the premium over cheaper soundhole pickups that sound like electric guitars. For players who need the convenience of a removable soundhole pickup but won't accept typical soundhole pickup tone, the M1 Active is the answer.

4. Fishman Rare Earth Soundhole Pickup
Best balanced soundhole pickup, warm and clear with excellent output

The Fishman Rare Earth is the premier conventional magnetic soundhole pickup. Rather than trying to blend in body resonance (like the LR Baggs M1), the Rare Earth is engineered to be the finest possible magnetic string pickup — and succeeds impressively. The neodymium rare earth magnets (hence the name) provide higher output with lower magnetic drag on the strings, which means the guitar resonates more freely and sustains better than with conventional alnico or ceramic magnets.

The tone is warm, full, and balanced. It sounds like a very good electric guitar with acoustic-appropriate voicing — not quite the natural bloom of an internal transducer, but musical and appropriate for strumming and flatpicking in live situations. Installation requires no guitar modification. At around $129, it's priced between budget soundhole pickups and the premium M1 Active. For players who want excellent soundhole pickup tone at a practical price, the Rare Earth is the most recommended option in its category.

5. Seymour Duncan Woody SA-3SC
Best budget soundhole pickup, single-coil clarity at an accessible price

The Seymour Duncan Woody SA-3SC brings Seymour Duncan's electric guitar pickup expertise to the acoustic world at an accessible price. The single-coil design delivers clear, bright articulation that suits strumming, fingerpicking, and chord work. The passive design requires no battery and installs in seconds with no guitar modification — just slide it into the soundhole, tighten the thumbscrews, and play.

The tone is bright and clear with good string-to-string balance, though it lacks the warmth and body resonance of more sophisticated designs. At around $55-75, the Woody SA-3SC is the affordable entry point into quality acoustic amplification. It's best suited for players who want to plug in occasionally rather than dedicated acoustic performers who need the most natural sound possible. A great choice for open mic nights, casual performances, or players who want to try acoustic amplification before investing in a permanent installed pickup system.

Buying Guide

How to Choose the Right Acoustic Pickup

  • Natural tone is your priority: LR Baggs Lyric or K&K Pure Mini. Both require installation and both deliver the most natural, acoustic-sounding amplified tone available. The Lyric handles louder stages better; the K&K is more affordable and beloved by fingerpickers.
  • No guitar modification: LR Baggs M1 Active, Fishman Rare Earth, or Seymour Duncan Woody. All three snap into the soundhole without any permanent changes to your guitar.
  • Loud stage environments: Soundhole pickups (M1 Active, Rare Earth, Woody) handle high stage volumes with the least feedback risk. The LR Baggs Lyric uses DSP to extend feedback headroom further than typical internal mics.
  • Budget choice: Seymour Duncan Woody at $55-75 is the obvious pick. It won't sound as natural as premium options but it works, requires no installation, and costs less than any other option on this list.
  • Do you need a preamp/DI? If you're buying the K&K Pure Mini (passive), budget for a quality preamp DI (LR Baggs Para DI or similar). The Fishman Rare Earth and LR Baggs M1 Active include preamps. The Lyric requires a phantom-powered or battery-powered preamp.
Final Verdict

Which Acoustic Pickup Should You Buy?

For the most natural acoustic tone, the LR Baggs Lyric is the benchmark — but it requires professional installation and investment. The K&K Pure Mini is the enthusiast's choice at a lower price, delivering outstanding body resonance for fingerpickers and acoustic purists. The LR Baggs M1 Active wins for players who need a removable soundhole pickup with genuine acoustic character. The Fishman Rare Earth is the best conventional soundhole pickup. And the Seymour Duncan Woody SA-3SC is the smart, affordable entry point for players who want to amplify occasionally without commitment.
