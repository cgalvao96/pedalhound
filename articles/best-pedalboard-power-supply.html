Best Pedalboard Power Supply 2026: Stop the Hum for Good

A bad power supply is the most common source of noise on a pedalboard, and it's also one of the most overlooked purchases. Most players spend hundreds on pedals and then power them with a $20 daisy-chain from a power brick — then spend hours chasing hum they think is coming from the pedals themselves. A quality isolated power supply solves this problem permanently.

This guide covers the most important concepts (isolated outputs, milliamp requirements, voltage options) and ranks the best power supplies for boards of different sizes.

Isolated vs Daisy Chain: Why It Matters

A daisy-chain power supply runs all your pedals off a single electrical output, sharing the ground connection across all pedals. This is fine for small boards with pedals that don't generate noise — but in practice, it creates ground loops that manifest as hum, buzz, or high-frequency interference. Digital pedals are particularly problematic in daisy-chain setups, as they generate switching noise that bleeds into analog pedals sharing the same power source.

An isolated power supply gives each pedal its own separate electrical circuit with its own ground reference. This eliminates ground loops entirely. Pedals that hummed constantly in a daisy-chain setup are silent. The difference is not subtle — it's the difference between sounding like a professional and sounding like you're playing through a faulty cable.

Understanding Milliamps and Voltage

Every pedal has a current draw specified in milliamps (mA). Your power supply must provide at least that amount per output — more is fine, less will cause the pedal to malfunction or not power on. Digital pedals (loopers, reverbs, delays, multi-effects) typically draw 100–500mA. Analog pedals draw 20–100mA. Check your pedals' specs and add up the total before choosing a power supply.

Most pedals run on 9V DC negative-tip. Some run on 12V or 18V — older Boss pedals, Voodoo Lab pedals, and some boutique units. A good power supply includes outputs at multiple voltages, or allows voltage adjustment per output.

Top Pedalboard Power Supplies Ranked

1. Strymon Ojai R30
Best compact isolated supply — 5 outputs, ultra-quiet
9.4 / 10

The Strymon Ojai R30 is the best compact pedalboard power supply available. Five fully isolated 9V outputs at 500mA each handle most digital pedals without issue, and the ultra-low noise floor (under 10μV) is genuinely exceptional — in testing with 12 pedals including three digital units, the noise floor was effectively inaudible. The compact enclosure mounts easily under a pedalboard using included hardware.

The R30 can be expanded with additional Ojai units using the Strymon Zuma as a hub, making it a scalable solution for large boards. At $165, it's an investment that pays for itself the first time you perform without hum.

2. Voodoo Lab Pedal Power 2 Plus
Best versatile supply — handles 9V, 12V, and 18V
9.1 / 10

The Pedal Power 2 Plus has been the professional standard for isolated pedalboard power since 2001 and remains an excellent choice. Eight isolated outputs cover 9V, 12V, and 18V pedals, with two "high-current" outputs for digital pedals drawing up to 250mA. The "sag" output simulates a dying battery for vintage fuzz pedals that benefit from reduced voltage.

The included cables and adaptors handle virtually every connector type. It's bulkier than the Ojai and requires an external wall-wart power supply, but the voltage flexibility makes it the best choice for boards with mixed voltage requirements.

Buying Guide

How to Choose the Right Power Supply

  • Count your outputsAdd up all the pedals on your board, then buy a supply with at least that many isolated outputs plus one or two spare for future expansion.
  • Check milliamp requirementsAdd up the mA draw of all your pedals. Each output must meet or exceed the individual pedal's requirement. Digital pedals (delays, reverbs, loopers) are often 100–500mA.
  • Check voltage requirementsMost pedals are 9V, but some boutique and older pedals require 12V or 18V. The Voodoo Lab and Truetone options handle these; the Strymon Ojai does not.
  • Board sizeSmall boards (under 6 pedals) — Strymon Ojai. Medium boards (6–10 pedals) — MXR ISO-Brick. Large boards (10+ pedals or mixed voltages) — Truetone CS12.
  • Avoid daisy chains entirelyOnce you've heard an isolated supply, you'll never go back. The noise reduction is not a subtle improvement — it's transformative.
Final Verdict

Which Power Supply Should You Buy?

For most players with small-to-medium all-9V boards, the Strymon Ojai R30 is the best purchase. Its noise floor is exceptional, its build quality is excellent, and it's compact enough to mount under any pedalboard. For boards with mixed voltage requirements, the Voodoo Lab Pedal Power 2 Plus or MXR ISO-Brick offer the flexibility the Ojai lacks. And for large, complex boards, the Truetone CS12 is the most complete single-unit solution available.
